A hydrogen orbital is a wavefunction, not a path around the nucleus. The cloud shows where a position measurement is more likely; the moving tracers reveal the flow of probability.

02 The equation behind the cloud

These are bound-state solutions for one electron in the Coulomb field of a fixed proton. Relativity, spin, external fields and interactions with other electrons are omitted.

\[\mathrm i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\nabla^2-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}\right]\Psi\]
\[E_n=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_ea_0^2n^2}\approx-\frac{13.606\;\mathrm{eV}}{n^2}\]
\[a_0=\frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar^2}{m_ee^2}\approx0.0529\;\mathrm{nm}\]

Here \(\hbar\) is the reduced Planck constant, \(m_e\) the electron mass, \(e\) the positive elementary charge and \(\varepsilon_0\) the vacuum permittivity. The Bohr radius \(a_0\) is the unit of distance.

03 Radial shells and nodes

Separation in spherical coordinates splits the wavefunction into radial, angular and time factors. \(\theta\) is measured from \(+z\); \(\varphi\) turns around \(z\) from \(+x\) toward \(+y\).

\[\Psi_{n\ell m}(r,\theta,\varphi,t)=R_{n\ell}(r)Y_\ell^m(\theta,\varphi)\,\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i E_nt/\hbar}\]
\[R_{n\ell}(r)=\sqrt{\left(\frac{2}{na_0}\right)^3\frac{(n-\ell-1)!}{2n(n+\ell)!}}\;\mathrm e^{-u/2}u^\ell L_{n-\ell-1}^{\,2\ell+1}(u)\]
\[\begin{aligned}u&=\frac{2r}{na_0},\\[0.5em]L_k^\alpha(u)&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\frac{(k+\alpha)!}{(k-j)!(\alpha+j)!j!}\,u^j.\end{aligned}\]

\(L_k^\alpha\) is a generalized Laguerre polynomial; here \(k=n-\ell-1\) and \(\alpha=2\ell+1\) are nonnegative integers. Its roots create \(n-\ell-1\) radial nodes: spherical surfaces with zero density. Increasing \(n\) generally extends the cloud; the characteristic radius grows like \(n^2a_0\).

04 Spherical harmonics, phase and basis

For \(0\le q\le\ell\), the normalized complex angular function is

\[Y_\ell^q(\theta,\varphi)=\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell+1)(\ell-q)!}{4\pi(\ell+q)!}}\;P_\ell^q(\cos\theta)\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i q\varphi}\]
\[Y_\ell^{-q}=(-1)^q\bigl(Y_\ell^q\bigr)^*\]

\(P_\ell^q\) is the associated Legendre function on \([-1,1]\), including the Condon–Shortley sign:

\[P_\ell^q(x)=\frac{(-1)^q}{2^\ell\ell!}(1-x^2)^{q/2}\frac{\mathrm d^{\ell+q}}{\mathrm dx^{\ell+q}}(x^2-1)^\ell\]

Complex basis: phase winds \(m\) times around \(z\). Opposite \(m\) values have identical densities and opposite current. The global time phase changes no probabilities.

Real basis: combine \(\pm q\) to make the familiar directional lobes. The signed index selects cosine \((+)\) or sine \((- )\), rather than a definite \(L_z\).

\[Y_{\ell,m}^{\mathrm{real}}=\begin{cases}Y_\ell^0,&m=0,\\[0.5em]\sqrt{2}\,\operatorname{Re}Y_\ell^m,&m>0,\\[0.5em]\sqrt{2}\,\operatorname{Im}Y_\ell^{|m|},&m<0.\end{cases}\]

Real orbitals have \(\ell\) angular nodal surfaces. Complex orbitals have \(\ell-|m|\) polar nodal surfaces, with an additional zero on the \(z\) axis when \(|m|>0\). NIST DLMF · Spherical harmonics ↗

05 Probability: volume versus radius

The Born rule turns a complex amplitude into probability density. A probability comes from integrating over a volume, not from reading the value at one point.

\[\rho=|\Psi|^2,\qquad P(V)=\int_V\rho\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf r,\qquad\int_{\mathbb R^3}\rho\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf r=1\]
\[\mathrm dP=\bigl|R_{n\ell}(r)\bigr|^2\bigl|Y_\ell^m(\theta,\varphi)\bigr|^2r^2\sin\theta\;\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi\]
\[P_r(r)=r^2\bigl|R_{n\ell}(r)\bigr|^2,\qquad\int_0^\infty P_r(r)\,\mathrm dr=1\]

The factor \(r^2\) accounts for the increasing area of spherical shells. For 1s, local density is greatest at the nucleus, while the radial probability peaks at \(a_0\).

06 What the moving particles mean

Probability current measures probability flowing through an area per unit time. It depends on the spatial gradient of phase, not only brightness.

\[\mathbf j=\frac{\hbar}{m_e}\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\Psi^*\nabla\Psi\right),\qquad\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf j=0\]

For a complex hydrogen eigenstate, the phase factor \(\mathrm e^{\mathrm i m\varphi}\) gives an azimuthal current. Away from nodes, its flow field is

\[\mathbf v=\frac{\mathbf j}{\rho}=\frac{\hbar m}{m_er\sin\theta}\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\varphi\]
\[\frac{\mathrm d\varphi}{\mathrm dt}=\frac{\hbar m}{m_er^2\sin^2\theta},\qquad\frac{\mathrm dr}{\mathrm dt}=\frac{\mathrm d\theta}{\mathrm dt}=0\]

Changing the sign of \(m\) reverses circulation. For \(m=0\), or a stationary real orbital, the current is zero. A stationary cloud can therefore have a circulating current while its density stays constant.

Tracers illustrate this flow field; they are not measured electron trajectories. Animation time is rescaled for visibility and is not a laboratory clock. At a node, \(\mathbf j/\rho\) is undefined. MIT · Probability current and conservation ↗

07 From mathematics to the screen

The renderer evaluates the analytic orbital in three dimensions, then accumulates samples along viewing rays. Density mode uses brightness to expose structure; the single-orbital phase view colors the complex angle \(\arg\Psi\). Density keeps its blue-to-white palette. Phase uses a vibrant cyclic rainbow. The colors at \(-\pi\) and \(+\pi\) match, and hue transitions remain smooth as the angle wraps around a full turn. In phase mode, hue represents an angle, not a probability.

Shading is a visual mapping. State-dependent exposure and nonlinear brightness reveal faint outer lobes. Display brightness is not an absolute probability scale, and equal brightness across different orbitals does not imply equal density.

Sculpted particles add depth. Each probability sample is drawn as a shaded spherical surface, with darker blue at low density and pale blue or white at high density. Surface lighting and bright centers illustrate the distribution; they are not light emitted by the atom, and the spheres are not solid electrons. Ultra adds finer volume sampling, more sculpted particles, layered highlights and luminous rims. This stylized lighting is computed locally on the GPU. Adaptive quality monitors frame rate, steps down under sustained load and cautiously adds detail when there is room.

Cutaways reveal existing structure. Half, wedge and slice controls hide parts of the rendered volume. They do not collapse, renormalize or physically change the quantum state.

Quality changes sampling. More ray steps and pixels improve detail; more tracers sample the same flow. Finite viewing bounds omit diffuse outer tails, a soft edge fade avoids a visible cutoff, and finite sampling can soften narrow nodes.

08 One wavelength, many state pairs

In this spin-free Coulomb model, energy depends only on \(n\). Every allowed \(\ell,m\) within a shell has the same energy. A shell contains \(n^2\) independent spatial states; the real and complex orbitals in the atlas are two bases of that same space.

\[g_n=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1)=n^2,\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{6}n^2=91\]

Let \(a=n_{\mathrm{lower}}\) and \(b=n_{\mathrm{upper}}\), with \(a\lt b\). Each shell pair has one positive energy gap and one vacuum wavelength in the displayed approximation:

\[\begin{aligned}E_n&=-\frac{13.605693\,\mathrm{eV}}{n^2},\\[0.5em]\Delta E_{ab}&=13.605693\,\mathrm{eV}\left(\frac{1}{a^2}-\frac{1}{b^2}\right),\\[0.5em]\lambda_{ab}&=\frac{hc}{\Delta E_{ab}},\qquad hc=1239.841984332\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}.\end{aligned}\]

The 15 shell pairs through \(n=6\) have 15 distinct gaps. Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett and Pfund end at \(a=1,2,3,4,5\), respectively. The spectrum stops at \(n=6\); each physical series continues beyond the displayed lines toward its series limit.

\[N_{\mathrm{lines}}=\binom{6}{2}=15\]

A fixed \(\Delta n=b-a\) groups several wavelengths; it does not determine a wavelength. For example, \(1\leftrightarrow2\) and \(5\leftrightarrow6\) both change \(n\) by one, but have very different gaps. A selected wavelength identifies the shell pair, then the subshell and magnetic-channel controls identify its spatial states.

Counting the allowed pairs. For a fixed \(a\lt b\), the electric-dipole rule \(\Delta\ell=\pm1\) permits \(a\) upward-\(\ell\) branches and \(a-1\) downward-\(\ell\) branches. A branch connecting \(\ell\) and \(\ell+1\) contains \(3(2\ell+1)\) magnetic channels, one for every allowed lower \(m\) and \(q=0,\pm1\).

\[\begin{aligned}N_{\mathrm{subshell}}(a,b)&=2a-1,\\[0.4em]N_{\mathrm{channels}}(a,b)&=3\!\sum_{\ell=0}^{a-1}(2\ell+1)+3\!\sum_{\ell=1}^{a-1}(2\ell-1)\\[0.4em]&=3\left[a^2+(a-1)^2\right].\end{aligned}\]

09 Selection rules, polarization and coupling

Light couples to the electron through its electric dipole. In the long-wavelength approximation, \(\hat{\mathbf d}=-e\mathbf r\) and the interaction is \(\hat H'=-\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\mathbf E(t)\). Its spherical spatial components are

\[r_0=z,\qquad r_{\pm1}=\mp\frac{x\pm\mathrm i y}{\sqrt2}=r\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}\,Y_1^{\pm1},\qquad r_0=r\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}\,Y_1^0.\]

Write the lower state as \(|a,\ell,m\rangle\) and the upper state as \(|b,L,M\rangle\). The channel label is always \(q=M-m\): the change in the atom's magnetic quantum number during absorption. Nonzero dipole coupling requires

\[L=\ell\pm1,\qquad q=M-m\in\{-1,0,+1\},\qquad |m|\le\ell,\quad |M|\le L.\]

Parity changes because the dipole operator is odd. There is no additional electric-dipole rule restricting \(\Delta n\). Same-shell changes have zero photon energy in this model and are not finite-wavelength spectral lines.

\(\sigma^+\)\(q=+1\)Circular component that raises the atom's \(m\)
\(\sigma^-\)\(q=-1\)Circular component that lowers the atom's \(m\)

These labels specify coupling components, not an observer-dependent clockwise or counterclockwise arrow. For propagation along \(z\), the two circular polarizations address the \(\sigma\) channels; a \(\pi\) field needs a propagation direction transverse to \(z\). Other field orientations are combinations of components. During emission along the same reversible channel, the atom changes \(m\) by \(-q\).

Radial overlap and angular coupling. Separating variables gives one radial integral for a subshell pair and one angular factor for each magnetic channel:

\[\begin{aligned}\langle bLM|r_q|a\ell m\rangle&=I_{a\ell,bL}\,\mathcal A_{\ell m,L M}^{(q)},\\[0.5em]I_{a\ell,bL}&=\int_0^\infty R_{bL}(r)R_{a\ell}(r)r^3\,\mathrm dr,\\[0.5em]\mathcal A_{\ell m,L M}^{(q)}&=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}\int (Y_L^M)^*Y_1^qY_\ell^m\,\mathrm d\Omega.\end{aligned}\]

The radial functions are normalized as in section 03. The angular integral is a Gaunt coefficient; equivalently, using Wigner \(3j\) symbols,

\[\mathcal A_{\ell m,L M}^{(q)}=(-1)^M\sqrt{(2L+1)(2\ell+1)}\begin{pmatrix}L&1&\ell\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}L&1&\ell\\-M&q&m\end{pmatrix}.\]

The code evaluates its square directly. For \(L=\ell+1\), set \(D=(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)\):

\[\left|\mathcal A_{\ell m,\ell+1,m+q}^{(q)}\right|^2=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(\ell+1)^2-m^2}{D},&q=0,\\[0.8em]\dfrac{(\ell+m+1)(\ell+m+2)}{2D},&q=+1,\\[0.8em]\dfrac{(\ell-m+1)(\ell-m+2)}{2D},&q=-1.\end{cases}\]

The \(L=\ell-1\) result follows by interchanging the angular states and reversing \(q\); the squared magnitude is unchanged. The radial integrals use converged Simpson quadrature in units of \(a_0\), cached once per subshell pair.

What the strength bars mean. Each bar measures the squared spatial dipole matrix element. Its relative value is normalized against the strongest magnetic channel on the same wavelength, including all subshells and all three polarization components:

\[S_c=\left|I_{a\ell,bL}\right|^2\left|\mathcal A_c\right|^2,\qquad s_c=\frac{S_c}{\max_{c'\in\mathcal C_{ab}}S_{c'}}.\]

\(S_c\) has units of \(a_0^2\); multiplying it by \(e^2\) gives the squared electric-dipole moment. Filters do not change the denominator. A relative value of one identifies the strongest channel at that wavelength; it is not a 100% transition probability.

Observed line brightness also depends on the number of atoms in each state, the illuminating field, polarization, frequency, decay and observation geometry. Spontaneous-emission rates scale as \(\omega^3\) times dipole strength; emitted power also includes photon energy and upper-state population. Summing channels requires a specified population model. The spectrum therefore presents coupling strengths, not predicted measured intensities.

10 Driven motion between two states

A resonant, coherent field can exchange population between two coupled states. For an ideal isolated pair, initially in the lower state and driven without decay, the rotating-wave approximation gives

\[P_u(t)=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Omega t}{2}\right),\qquad P_l(t)=1-P_u(t).\]

For a monochromatic field \(\mathbf E(t)=\operatorname{Re}[\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}\,\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\omega t}]\), define \(\Omega=|\langle u|\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}|l\rangle|/\hbar\). Thus physical Rabi rates depend on field amplitude, polarization and the channel's dipole matrix element. With one matched field component held fixed, \(\Omega\) scales as \(\sqrt{S_c}\).

The display uses a common, ten-second cycle. Its speed makes different shapes easy to compare and does not predict physical Rabi times or scale playback by the strength bars. The rising population illustrates absorption; the falling population illustrates stimulated emission. The repeating animation is not repeated spontaneous decay.

The coherent wavefunction contains two energy-dependent phases:

\[\Psi(\mathbf r,t)=c_l(t)\psi_l(\mathbf r)\mathrm e^{-\mathrm iE_lt/\hbar}+c_u(t)\psi_u(\mathbf r)\mathrm e^{-\mathrm iE_ut/\hbar}.\]

Its density has an interference term oscillating at the optical difference frequency. The transition cloud averages over that fast oscillation while retaining the slow populations:

\[\begin{aligned}|\Psi|^2={}&|c_l|^2|\psi_l|^2+|c_u|^2|\psi_u|^2\\&+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left[c_l^*c_u\psi_l^*\psi_u\,\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i(E_u-E_l)t/\hbar}\right],\\[0.7em]\bar\rho(\mathbf r,t)={}&\bigl[1-P_u(t)\bigr]|\psi_l(\mathbf r)|^2+P_u(t)|\psi_u(\mathbf r)|^2.\end{aligned}\]

The averaging assumes an interval long compared with the optical period and short compared with the population cycle. The two normalized distributions share one physical coordinate scale; a common visual exposure is applied after mixing. Both probabilities add to one, and the integrated density remains one.

Every selected channel uses the complex magnetic basis \(|n,\ell,m\rangle\). Opposite signs of \(m\) have identical density, so some different channels look the same in this averaged view even though their phase, current or polarization coupling differs. The state labels and strength bars retain that distinction. The atlas's real orbitals are superpositions of the complex states, not additional channels to be added to the count.

Two-state scope. With degenerate magnetic sublevels, a real applied field can couple several states simultaneously. This view isolates one matrix element at a time; a wavelength alone does not prepare a unique channel. It omits multilevel coherent dynamics, spontaneous decay, decoherence, saturation in an ensemble and line broadening. Electric-quadrupole, magnetic-dipole, multiphoton and ionization processes are outside this electric-dipole, bound-state model. A dipole-forbidden transition can still occur by another mechanism.

Ultraviolet and infrared lines use illustrative interface colors, not visible photon colors. The orbital cloud is a probability distribution; it does not depict an electron traveling between two surfaces. MIT · Coherently driven two-level systems ↗

11 Viewing a transition: basis, phase and current

The transition viewer starts in Complex · Density · Cloud, with the whole distribution visible. Its appearance controls use the same blue density palette, cyclic phase palette, cutaways and particle shading as the orbital explorer. The selected spectral line, channel and population cycle remain independent of the camera, intensity and quality settings.

Complex states and real-basis previews. Complex mode displays the two magnetic eigenstates named by the selected channel. Real orbitals replaces each endpoint by its normalized real counterpart from section 04: the positive index selects a cosine combination, the negative index a sine combination. This is a preview of different basis shapes in the same energy shells, not a claim that their coupling equals the selected complex channel's coupling.

The wavelength is unchanged because these combinations have the same shell energies. The displayed selection rules, polarization and strength continue to refer to the named complex states. A physical transition calculation between real combinations would transform the full matrix of dipole amplitudes before taking its squared magnitude:

\[D_{\mathrm{real}}^{(q)}=U_u^\dagger D_{\mathrm{complex}}^{(q)}U_l.\]

The columns of \(U_l\) and \(U_u\) contain the real basis vectors expressed in their complex magnetic bases. The signed index in a real preview is not a definite value of angular momentum along \(z\). Real combinations add no independent states to the catalog.

Component phase is a color convention. After optical averaging, the density is a population-weighted sum. This averaged representation has no unique single wavefunction phase to color; it does not assert that the underlying driven state has physically decohered. The cloud therefore colors each component's phase separately, then blends its colors according to local density. With \(p_l=1-P\), \(p_u=P\), \(\rho_k=|\psi_k|^2\), and a cyclic color map \(F\), the unshaded volume color is

\[\mathbf C(\mathbf r)=\frac{p_l\rho_l(\mathbf r)F\!\bigl(\phi_l(\mathbf r)\bigr)+p_u\rho_u(\mathbf r)F\!\bigl(\phi_u(\mathbf r)\bigr)}{p_l\rho_l(\mathbf r)+p_u\rho_u(\mathbf r)},\qquad\bar\rho(\mathbf r)>0.\]

Here \(\phi_k\) is the phase of one component in the display's chosen convention; each component particle carries its own phase color. Zero-density regions contribute no color. The palette wraps smoothly at \(-\pi\) and \(+\pi\). Blended colors are not the phase of a coherent superposition and do not restore the interference term omitted in section 10. Changing a component's arbitrary overall phase changes its colors without changing its density or coupling strength.

Circulation is separate from population transfer. The tracers sample each endpoint distribution and circle according to that component's stationary current. Their population weights change with the cycle. They illustrate the component circulation field

\[\mathbf j_{\mathrm{circ}}=(1-P)\mathbf j_l+P\mathbf j_u,\qquad\mathbf j_k=\frac{\hbar}{m_e}\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\psi_k^*\nabla\psi_k\right).\]

Each stationary complex component follows the azimuthal flow in section 06. Opposite magnetic signs circulate in opposite directions. A stationary real component, or a component with \(m=0\), has zero circulation. The moving samples do not travel from one orbital to the other.

This overlay does not solve the full local probability transport of the driven atom. The distinction can be seen directly: each stationary current is divergence-free, whereas the chosen population interpolation changes the local density, giving

\[\frac{\partial\bar\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf j_{\mathrm{circ}}=\dot P\left(\rho_u-\rho_l\right).\]

The right-hand side integrates to zero because both endpoint densities are normalized. It records the change of the displayed component weights; it is not creation or loss of an electron. The full driven quantum evolution conserves probability locally, but its transfer current and fast coherences are outside this averaged visualization. The circulation overlay alone must not be interpreted as that full current.

Two independent visual clocks. The population slider and playback control set the ten-second absorption–stimulated-emission cycle. Flow speed and its pause control set the illustrative circulation and phase-color clock. Pausing one does not pause the other. These clocks are rescaled for viewing and do not measure physical transition times or relative optical phase.

Geometry and lighting. Cloud mode integrates the mixed density along viewing rays. Sculpted particles draw samples from the endpoint distributions as shaded spheres, weighted by their populations. Sphere sizes, highlights and dense bright cores aid depth perception; they are not electron sizes or emitted light. Half, wedge and slice controls hide selected geometry without changing or renormalizing either state. Rotation, zoom, coordinate axes and adaptive quality change the view, not the spectrum or coupling calculation.
