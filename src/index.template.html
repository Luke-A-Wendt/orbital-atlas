The physics of a spectral line

In this nonrelativistic hydrogen model, energy depends only on the shell number. A spectral line is one energy gap, shared by every allowed orbital and magnetic-state pair between those shells.

Photon energy = upper-state energy − lower-state energy

Electric-dipole coupling requires a change of one in the orbital quantum number and a magnetic-number change of zero or one in either direction. The explorer enumerates all 15 shell pairs, 55 allowed orbital pairs and 465 allowed signed magnetic-state pairs through shell six. Spin, fine structure, external-field splitting and higher-order transitions are outside this model.

A resonant field can drive population into the upper state and back through stimulated emission. The cloud shows the optical-cycle-averaged density: the two orbital densities weighted by their populations.

Density = lower population × lower-state density + upper population × upper-state density

The animation illustrates driven population transfer. It does not trace an electron path or simulate spontaneous emission. Optical phase interference is averaged out. Each card is an independently scaled density cross section; the animated pair shares one spatial scale.

Wavelengths are vacuum values in the fixed-proton hydrogen model used throughout the atlas. Measured wavelengths include additional corrections. Visible line colors are approximate; ultraviolet and infrared lines use blue markers.
